Why Roofs in Akron Wear Out So Fast

Akron weather is hard on roofs. Snow blows in from Lake Erie in winter, summers are hot and humid, and storms can arrive with very strong winds. The biggest problem is the constant freezing and thawing. It happens around 60 to 80 times a year here. Water gets into tiny cracks, freezes, expands, and slowly pries shingles and flashing apart. In winter the same process builds ice dams along the edge of the roof, and the water trapped behind them can find its way inside. A roof in this part of Ohio has to be fitted with all of that in mind.

Questions People Often Ask Us

How long does a shingle roof last in Akron?** A good architectural shingle roof usually lasts about 22 to 28 years here if the attic is ventilated properly. Basic shingles wear out sooner, often in 15 to 18 years, because of the heavy snow and the strong summer sun.

Why do ice dams keep forming on my roof? Warm air from the attic melts the snow sitting on the upper part of the roof. The water runs down and freezes again at the cold edge, and a ridge of ice slowly builds up. Water pools behind that ridge and can slip under the shingles. Good insulation, proper ventilation, and a quality ice barrier all help to stop it.

Should I repair my roof or replace it?** It depends on the age of the roof and how far the damage has spread. If the roof is under 15 years old and the problem sits in one small area, a repair usually makes sense. If the roof is over 20 years old, or it leaks in several places at once, replacing it often works out cheaper than paying for repair after repair.

Do I need a permit to replace a roof in Akron? Yes. The city and the county both ask for permits for a full roof replacement. A good contractor sorts the paperwork out for you and makes sure the work passes inspection at the end.

How can I tell if a storm has damaged my roof?** Look for shingles lying in the yard, dark patches where the surface granules have been knocked off, and any new leaks after heavy rain. Some damage is hard to spot from the ground, so it is worth getting the roof checked soon after a big storm.
